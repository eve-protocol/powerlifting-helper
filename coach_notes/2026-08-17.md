## Coaching assessment
- This was a good illness-modified performance, but only a partial hit of the written low-fatigue objective. Strength remained available across every movement; the main deviation was repeated 0.5-1.0 RPE drift rather than a collapse in output.
- High bar was the clearest win. The 125kg opener was comfortably below target and justified 130kg. The anchor then landed at @7.5, only half an RPE above the ceiling, while using a consistent competition-depth standard. Compared with 120kg x6 @6.5-7 last week, this confirms productive high-bar progression. It does not establish ATG progression because the work-set standard was competition depth.
- Paused bench was slightly hotter than intended but materially better than last week's suppressed five-rep session. The 112.5kg anchor was available for the triple at @7.5, and reducing to 110kg prevented a larger overshoot even though it did not restore the target range. This supports adequate pressing strength but does not justify adding fatigue before the competition-bench checkpoint.
- Pull-ups repeated the established pattern: +27.5kg is available for the first five but starts above today's target, +25kg restores the range initially, and a third set can drift materially. The final @8.5 set was unnecessary for a reduced-fatigue day; future reduced-volume work should end or reduce further after the first backoff reaches the ceiling.
- Cable-crunch execution takes priority over the nominal load and logged RPE. If 45kg causes lower-body movement and reduces abdominal loading, it fails the exercise intent even at a reported @7. Forty kilograms is the new default first-set reference on this cable. Use a controlled spinal curl, keep the pelvis and lower body fixed, and reduce further if those positions cannot be retained. Progress control and abdominal tension before load.

## Block trajectory impact

- Bench remains plausible for the 122.5kg x5 checkpoint. Today's paused triple supports sufficient strength, but both sets exceeded the @7 ceiling and illness remains a readiness variable. The benchmark should be earned from same-day setup, pause, symmetry, and warm-up speed rather than assumed from today's completion.
- Pull-up strength is maintained, but +27.5kg is not yet a repeatable three-set load at @7-7.5.
- The session broadly supports the volume-transfer thesis: performance survived a meaningful recovery stressor. It does not prove that the flu has resolved or authorize harder work in the next benchmark session.

## Technical theme to carry forward
- High bar: use one depth standard across all work reps; today's competition-depth standard was consistent and productive.
- Bench: settle in the final position, hold the pause, then drive back into the bench; do not chase the anchor when the opening set is already above the ceiling.
- Cable crunch: curl the trunk without letting the lower body lift. The best load is the heaviest one that preserves direct abdominal tension, currently 40kg or lower rather than 45kg.
