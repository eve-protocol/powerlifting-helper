## Coaching assessment
- **Squat was the strongest main-lift result.** The opener was only modestly above target, and the immediate reduction produced two stable backoff sets exactly at the top of range. The completed 157.5kg four remains consistent with the established positive squat-strength trajectory; it does not justify increasing later anchors because Week 1 is submaximal intensification rather than a benchmark.
- Excess depth is a useful technical observation but should not be corrected by attempting to squat marginally high. The next objective is a decisive reversal as soon as the hip crease clearly passes the top of the knee, while maintaining tension and the cue: **Knees forward for the first inch; chest and hips rise together.** Video is needed to determine whether depth is materially contributing to the perceived difficulty or torso pattern.
- **Bench underperformed the written prescription.** Stopping 122.5kg after three repetitions at @8.5 was the correct fatigue-management decision; forcing the fourth would likely have turned a submaximal session into a grind. The backoffs show meaningful within-session fatigue because 115kg rose from @7.5 to @8.5.
- Squat-before-bench is a plausible contributor, and adapting to competition order is part of the block objective rather than an excuse. However, this was also the third consecutive pressing day with at least one set above target: Monday paused bench reached @8.5, Tuesday incline dumbbells reached @9.5, and today's competition bench again exceeded range. Current evidence therefore supports accumulated pressing fatigue plus unfamiliar meet-order sequencing, not a conclusion that competition-bench strength has disappeared.
- Pull-ups were a clean secondary-goal success. Repeating +27.5kg worked today and both sets remained controlled.
- Reverse hyperextensions were too hard for their role. The exercise was intended as low-fatigue machine calibration; @8 work after two competition lifts added fatigue without serving that purpose.
