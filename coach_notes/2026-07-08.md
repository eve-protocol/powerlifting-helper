## Coaching assessment
- This was a successful heavy low-bar day with one important nuance: the logged RPE says the top single was slightly hotter than prescribed, but the technical outcome was better than the number alone suggests.
- The 170kg x1 @9 is not a throwaway overshoot. It is the heaviest low-bar squat of the block so far, 5kg above last week's 165kg x1 @8, and the video showed a completed, continuous rep with better ownership than the recent low-bar pattern often has at high load. Ren's live report that driving traps into the bar helped prevent the hip-dominant lift is useful coaching data. That cue should be kept.
- Still, the @9 matters. The session should be interpreted as a productive top exposure, not a reason to accelerate the peak. The original goal was 1 @8-8.5. If the same 170kg rep had been cleaner and closer to @8.5, it would strongly support a more aggressive Week 3 squat single. At @9, it supports progress but argues for tight decision rules next time.
- The backoff work was excellent. 162.5kg x1 @8 exactly matched the second single target, and 155kg x2 @7 exactly matched the double target. This is a good sign: the top single did not wreck the session, and the backoffs reinforced the intended range rather than turning into a fatigue spiral.
- Accessory execution drifted hot on the second sets. T-bar row at 67.5kg x10 @8 was only slightly above target, but leg curl at 35kg x10 @8.5 was too hot for this peaking slot. Neither is a major concern, but lower-body accessories should not add meaningful hamstring fatigue after a heavy squat single during Week 2.

## Block trajectory impact
- Peaking Block v7 remains on track for the 180kg squat goal. A 170kg single at @9 is not enough by itself to project an easy 180kg, but compared with 165kg x1 @8 last week and the cleaner technical report today, it supports the idea that strength and position are moving in the right direction.
- The squat rebuild cue is evolving. "Keep knees forward for the first inch" remains the baseline cue, but today added a useful heavy-load cue: drive traps into the bar while keeping chest with hips. That appears to help Ren stay connected to the bar instead of letting the hips take over early.
- Week 2's process warning still applies. The top single was a small intentional push beyond the conservative call. Because the backoffs landed correctly, this was contained. Do not let this become permission to take extra proof attempts on heavy bench or heavy deadlift later this week.
- Tomorrow's heavy bench should not be affected by today's squat except as a general fatigue note. Saturday's heavy deadlift should treat today's 170kg squat as meaningful lower-body stress; pull selection should stay technical and not chase load if the setup or lockout gets loose.

## Technical theme to carry forward
- Squat: drive traps into the bar, then chest with hips out of the hole.
- The goal is not just standing up with 170kg+; it is keeping the first inch from turning into a hip-first good morning.
- Accessories: first set can calibrate, but second set should land in target instead of jumping straight past it.

## Next-time guidance
- Next heavy low-bar exposure should use today's 170kg x1 @9 as a useful anchor but not a required minimum. A future jump above 170kg should require a bridge single that is clearly faster and cleaner than today's 160kg, plus no hip-first shift on the top single.

- Backoff double at 155kg is validated for 2 @7 after a 170kg top single. Keep 155kg as the default unless the top single is unusually easy.
- T-bar row: 65kg is too easy for the target, but 67.5kg may be slightly hot after heavy squat. Use 67.5kg if first set warm-up suggests it will stay <=@7.5, otherwise use 65kg with stricter tempo.
