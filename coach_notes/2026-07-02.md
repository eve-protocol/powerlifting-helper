## Coaching assessment
- This was a useful but intentionally-not-repeatable bench day. The first set was off-plan and too hot for the Week 1 peaking prescription, but it gave a clear data point: 130kg x2 @9.5 is still there, matching last week's benchmark-level result rather than showing a new step forward.
- The important save was the response after the hot set. Dropping to 120kg for both doubles kept the rest of the bench work controlled. Both backoff doubles landed @7, slightly under the target range, but that was the correct tradeoff after overshooting the top exposure. A 122.5kg backoff would probably have turned the day into more fatigue with little extra peaking value.
- The handoff matters. 130kg x2 with a handoff is useful evidence for top-end pressing capacity, but it is not the same as validating meet-specific self-unrack/setup execution. Since the Strength Block v5 miss was partly about heavy setup and heavy-bar execution, the next bench priority should be making submaximal heavy singles clean and repeatable, not proving the same 130kg double again.
- Pull-ups were exactly where they should be. +25kg x5 @7.5 and @8 validates +25kg as the current default for two-set work at this target, but it should not be expanded into extra sets during the peak.
- Curls overshot the target at @8.5. This is minor in isolation, but it fits the recurring support-work risk from the weekly assessment: accessories can quietly become proof work. During the peak, curls should support elbows/upper back/pressing, not become a fatigue slot.

## Block trajectory impact
- Peaking Block v7 remains on track, but today slightly front-loaded bench stress. The 130kg double confirms that the pressing base is not gone and that a bench peak is still plausible, but it did not solve the actual peaking need: crisp heavy singles and reliable setup under meet-like conditions.
- The good news is that the overshoot was contained. The two 120kg doubles at @7 mean the session did not spiral into a true max-effort bench day. That matters because Saturday's heavy sumo is still the next key lower-body exposure, and the block's main active goals remain squat 180kg and sumo 200kg.

If the program calls for a single @8, take the single. Do not turn another Week 1 or Week 2 exposure into a repeat benchmark double just because the handoff feels good.

## Technical theme to carry forward
- Bench: own the setup before you own the weight.
- Handoff practice can be useful, but only if it does not change the day's intent.
- After a hot top set, backoffs should become boring immediately. Today that part was handled well.
