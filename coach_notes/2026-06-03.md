## Coaching assessment
- The synced squat result is objectively on target. The top single landed exactly in range, and all three backoff 4s matched the programmed RPE ranges.
- The subjective disappointment is still worth taking seriously because the comparison point is last week's 160kg x5 @10. Today's 160kg single and 145kg 4s do not show a strength crash, but they did not feel as reassuring as expected after the recent 5RM.
- The most likely explanation is accumulated posterior-chain fatigue plus movement-pattern specificity, not a simple loss of strength. Yesterday's paused deadlifts were productive, but Ren's hamstring fatigue showed up clearly on leg curls and may have made low-bar 4s feel more hip/back limited.
- The skipped high-bar exposure is a plausible contributor but not proven from one session. High bar has been serving as a knee-forward / quad-biased patterning slot. Without it this week, low-bar may have had less recent practice staying knee-forward and more room to drift back toward the hips/back-dominant pattern.
- The alternative hypothesis is also plausible: skipping high bar reduced quad/knee-dominant practice but also reduced local quad fatigue, which may partly explain the strong leg-extension performance. This does not mean high bar should be removed; it means today's accessory overperformance is not automatically evidence that skipping high bar helped the main squat.
- Leg extension was a strong positive. After 55kg x12 @6.5 in the prior heavy-low-bar slot, 65kg x10 @8 and @7.5 shows quad accessory capacity is ahead of expectation.
- Standing T-bar row also progressed cleanly. 62.5kg x10 landed exactly in the target range for repeated sets, which supports moving the row anchor up.
- Leg curl was the clearest fatigue signal of the day. 30kg was far too high after yesterday's paused deadlift exposure, producing @9 and then a missed-rep @9.5 set before 25kg finally landed correctly.
- The live load calls ended up correct after calibration, but the initial reasoning around backoff selection was too compressed. The useful anchor is now clearer: after a 160kg @7 single, 145kg x4 is the correct Week 1 low-bar backoff anchor when the target is @7-8.

## Block trajectory impact
- Strength Block v5 remains on track. This session supports the plan to build toward the 3RM goal because 160kg moved as a true @7 single and 145kg 4s were repeatable within target.
- The warning is not load capacity; it is whether the squat pattern degrades when high-bar work is absent and posterior-chain fatigue is present.
- Do not overreact after one week by immediately rewriting the block. Monitor next week's low-bar day and compare it against whether high-bar/knee-forward exposure is present, how deadlift fatigue feels, and whether 145-150kg backoffs stay crisp.
- If the same pattern repeats next week, reintroducing or protecting a high-bar / knee-dominant exposure becomes a stronger recommendation. The purpose would be positional reinforcement, not extra fatigue or extra squat volume for its own sake.
- Deadlift fatigue management should also be watched. Paused deadlifts are useful for the block goal, but they may create enough hamstring fatigue to distort the following day's heavy low-bar and leg curl performance.

## Technical theme to carry forward
- Squat cue: `knees forward, chest with hips`.
- Treat high-bar as a patterning tool: knee-forward tolerance and quad contribution, not simply another squat load to progress.
- On low-bar backoffs, success is repeatable 4s that do not turn into hips-back reps by rep 4.
