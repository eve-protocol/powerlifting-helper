## Coaching assessment
- This was a useful and mostly successful Week 1 heavy deadlift day. The synced log alone looks clean: 185kg x1 @8 and two 180kg doubles inside target. The live context makes it more nuanced, because there was a failed/blocked 185kg sequence before the successful single.
- The failed 185kg was not random weakness. It exposed the exact active coaching-focus problem: the pull can break or rise, but if the knees/hips do not create clearance and the bar-body connection is not preserved above the knee, lockout stalls. The "hips to ceiling" cue appears to have worked at lighter weights but turned into the wrong heavy-sumo solution at 185kg: hips up without enough knees-open / hips-through finish.
- The successful 185kg after the cue change is the best data point of the day. It validates that the strength is there when the top-half position is solved. It also supports keeping the block's 200kg deadlift goal alive, but only if future heavy singles use the correct cue from the start rather than discovering it after failed attempts.
- The 180kg doubles were stronger than expected after the missed attempts. They show that the corrected cue transferred immediately and that 180kg is currently a viable backoff double load. The second double at @8 is the correct ceiling for Week 1; no extra deadlift work was needed.
- Squat did its job. 145kg x3 x2 stayed in the light technical target after heavy pulling. This was not a squat strength day, and keeping it at 145kg was the right call to preserve Monday recovery.
- Bench also did its job. 115kg and 117.5kg triples were easy, but after Thursday's 130kg x2 @9.5 and today's deadlift work, stopping at two clean sets was better than chasing a heavier third set. This is a useful undershoot, not a missed bench slot.
