- Squat is the clearest programming failure. The block goal was 180kg, with a stretch of 182.5kg. The actual best make was 172.5kg, below the 2026-05-02 mock-meet squat of 175kg. The larger problem is that the 12-week plan made target outcomes look plausible on paper while the athlete under the bar was not actually prepared to realize them.

## Block trajectory impact
- The squat/deadlift rebuild thesis remains correct, but the implementation failed. Identifying knees-forward squat and deadlift lockout as priorities was not enough; the training did not convert those priorities into mock-meet performance.
- The next programming review should start from the failed result: 500kg total after 12 weeks, down 10kg from the 2026-05-02 total, with squat and deadlift both below the intended end-state.
- Health issues, travel, and equipment noise are context, not excuses. They should be analyzed for load-management implications, but they cannot be used to erase the programming verdict.
- The next block needs a more robust progression model with clearer performance checkpoints, earlier failure detection, and less reliance on late-cycle RPE optimism.

## Technical theme to carry forward
- Squat: the good-morning/hip-back tendency still limits heavy expression. The cue remains "knees forward for the first inch," but the next cycle must train it in a way that transfers to maximal low-bar attempts.
- Bench: PR strategy has to be decided before the session. If total-building is the goal, attempt selection must stay disciplined. If PR hunting is the goal, missed attempts are acceptable but cannot be used as clean strength diagnostics.
- Deadlift: "hips through before it slows" remains the correct cue. The 195kg miss shows this must happen earlier, not as a last-second lean-back rescue.
