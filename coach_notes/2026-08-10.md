## Coaching assessment
- This was a genuine low-readiness session, but the objective suppression was not uniform. Paused bench was clearly and substantially below expectation: 107.5kg x5 @9 compared with 105kg x5 at @7, @7, and @8 one week earlier. The successive reductions were correct and brought the final set down to @8, but pressing never returned to the target zone.
- High-bar performance was harder to interpret because the movement standard changed during the session. The opener mixed ATG and competition-depth reps and stopped one rep short. Once Ren used competition depth consistently, 120kg x6 landed at @6.5 and @7. Those sets show that general force production was not uniformly depressed, but they are not a direct progression from last week's fully ATG 115kg sevens.
- Pull-ups and cable crunches landed exactly within their target ranges. That further argues against treating the day as proof of a complete whole-body strength collapse, even though the subjective rubber-like feeling should be respected.
- The most defensible interpretation is an unexplained poor-readiness day with disproportionate pressing suppression. Accumulated Week 2 pressing fatigue is a plausible contributor, but the available data cannot establish a cause. One day does not justify a block-wide deload or benchmark rewrite.
- Load management was good. The pull-up test was reduced appropriately, bench load came down each set, and no bonus work was added. This is better execution discipline than the Week 2 overshoot pattern.

## Block trajectory impact
- Squat trajectory is not downgraded from this session, but today's competition-depth high bar cannot be used as evidence that the ATG developmental variation progressed normally. Future high-bar sessions should establish the intended depth before the first work set and reduce load if ATG cannot be retained.
- Bench confidence takes a short-term hit, not a structural downgrade. The 122.5kg x5 Week 4 checkpoint remains plausible from prior competition-bench work, but the next two pressing exposures must be watched closely. Repeated suppression would require lowering the benchmark expectation or reducing intervening pressing fatigue.
- Pull-up progression is supported: +27.5kg x5 @8 worked as a first-set test, while +25kg remained the repeatable load.
- The active accumulation thesis remains intact. The rest of Week 3 should use warm-up performance as a strict gate rather than trying to compensate for today's weakness.

## Technical theme to carry forward
- High bar: decide on ATG depth before loading; do not change the movement standard mid-set to preserve the number on the bar.
- Bench: settle in the final position and preserve the pause, but do not force the written anchor when pressing power is clearly absent.
- Readiness: distinguish the subjective whole-body sensation from lift-specific output. Use the first meaningful warm-up and first work set to decide, then reduce promptly.
