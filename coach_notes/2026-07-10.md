## Coaching assessment
- This was a mixed but recoverable Week 2 heavy deadlift day. The logged 180kg x1 @6.5 is a strong readiness sign, and the later 185kg x1 @8.5 still landed inside the planned top-single effort range. The problem is the decision sequence between them.
- The 190kg miss is not best interpreted as a pure strength failure. It came after bypassing the planned 185kg bridge on a thicker, smoother bar where grip and bar feel were worse than usual. That makes it a poor-quality peaking data point. The useful information is that 180kg was genuinely easy, but the environmental constraint and jump size made 190kg the wrong next attempt.
- The 185kg x1 @8.5 after the miss keeps the 200kg target alive but does not advance it as cleanly as a successful 187.5kg or 190kg single would have. It shows resilience and enough heavy-single capacity, but the day spent more fatigue and uncertainty than necessary for the same practical result.
- The missing programmed backoff double matters. Week 2 Day 5 was not only a max-ish single exposure; it was supposed to include a controlled double to preserve the heavy-deadlift groove without turning the day into a test. Because 190kg failed and 185kg became the final heavy rep, the session got a worse signal-to-fatigue ratio than planned.
- Squat was acceptable but slightly hot. 150kg x2 was likely fine, and 155kg x2 @6.5 is only a small overshoot, but after a failed deadlift attempt the better support choice would have been repeating 150kg or keeping 152.5kg as the ceiling.
- Bench was solid. The rolling bar note explains the instability, and 120kg x2 @6.5 landed exactly at the top of target. Bench continues to be the most stable lift in this peak.

## Block trajectory impact
- Peaking Block v7 remains on track, but Week 2 ends with a stronger process warning than Week 1. Squat and bench moved forward during the week, while deadlift strength is still present but the attempt-selection process created avoidable noise.
- The deadlift 200kg goal is not gone. A very easy 180kg and a made 185kg @8.5 after a failed 190kg still suggest enough base strength for a heavier Week 3 or mock-meet pull if the bar, grip, and top-half position are managed correctly.
- The block cannot afford another unplanned deadlift jump. The active coaching focus is not "prove floor speed"; it is maintaining lat/back/hip position and finishing cleanly above the knee. Grip/bar mismatch should make the day more conservative, not more aggressive.
- Week 3 should use calibrated bridges. If 180kg is easy again, the next step should be 185kg or 187.5kg depending on bar feel and lockout, not a direct jump to 190kg unless the session prescription and setup clearly justify it.

## Technical theme to carry forward
- Deadlift: bridge the attempt before you bet the day. Open knees, hips through, bar glued in.
- Squat: after heavy pulling, stay boring. Light low-bar should support the next week, not become a small extra challenge.
- Bench: keep the pause and press path stable even when the bar rolls.

## Next-time guidance

- Do not count a failed 190kg on a bad bar as proof that 190kg is unavailable. Count it as proof that setup conditions and attempt jumps matter.
- If a heavy deadlift miss happens, do not chase the same intent again. Take one corrected single only if it still fits the day, then either perform the planned backoff double at a conservative load or move on.
- For the next light low-bar after deadlift, 150-152.5kg is the useful range. 155kg should require the first set to be clearly below @5.5 and the deadlift portion to have stayed clean.
