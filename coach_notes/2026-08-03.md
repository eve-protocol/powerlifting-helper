## Coaching assessment
- The high-bar work was productive. The first 115kg set landed at the top of target, the second drifted only 0.5 RPE high, and the reduction to 112.5kg restored the final set to target. That is the set-to-set discipline Week 1 was missing.
- The absolute high-bar load should not be interpreted as evidence that Ren is unusually weak at the movement. His ATG execution adds range of motion and makes the lift less mechanically comparable with a competition-depth high-bar squat. For this developmental slot, the load is subordinate to the intended knee-forward quad stimulus, repeatable bracing, and clean chest/hip timing.
- ATG high bar is a valid hypertrophy choice when it is comfortable and controlled. The greater knee flexion and long quadriceps working range plausibly support the quad-focused purpose. There is no reason to force an artificial competition-depth stop on this variation merely to raise the number. The relevant progression metric is more load or reps at the same ATG standard and target RPE.
- The main technical guardrail is not depth itself. If the bottom position produces pelvic collapse, loss of brace, Achilles/knee aggravation, or a rebound that changes the intended movement, depth should be limited to the deepest controlled position. No such problem was reported today.
- Paused bench was acceptable for two sets but the third set at @8 was a clear overshoot. Starting at 105kg instead of forcing the 107.5kg anchor was correct; the better decision after the second set at @7 would have been a 102.5kg backoff. This is a fatigue-management issue, not evidence that paused-bench strength regressed.
- Pull-ups repeated the recent pattern: +22.5kg is appropriate for the opener but not yet for all three six-rep sets inside @7-7.5. The reduction to +20kg successfully restored the final set.
- Cable crunch landed exactly as programmed and supports moving forward from the prior 40kg exposure.

## Block trajectory impact
- Today supports the Week 2 accumulation objective. High-bar volume progressed substantially from Week 1 while retaining the deliberate ATG standard, and the final-set correction protected quality.
- The ATG standard should remain consistent across high-bar exposures so progress can be judged honestly. High-bar numbers should not be compared directly with comp-depth or low-bar numbers without accounting for the deeper ROM.
- The session modestly improves execution discipline: high-bar and pull-ups were corrected after overshoots, but paused bench still needed an earlier reduction. The block remains on track toward the Week 4 low-bar and bench checkpoints, with transfer still unproven.

## Technical theme to carry forward
- High bar: ATG is the standard; knees first, stay braced, and let chest and hips rise together.
- Paused bench: when the second set reaches the RPE ceiling, reduce before the third set.
- Pull-ups: +22.5kg remains a first-set load until repeated sixes stay inside target.
