## Variation monitoring requirement
- Monitor every developmental variation against its explicit intended limiter.
- A variation should be reviewed closely when any of the following occurs:
  - it completes two blocks without improving its intended technical or strength outcome;
  - the competition-lift limiter remains unchanged;
  - repeated exposures create disproportionate fatigue, pain, or session disruption;
  - progress occurs in the variation without useful transfer;
  - equipment changes make the old load reference unreliable.
- Current examples that require explicit transfer checks:
  - high-bar squat -> knee-forward contribution and better chest/hip timing in low bar;
  - paused-at-knee sumo -> maintained bar connection and a legal stacked competition lockout;
  - Spoto press -> bottom-range tension, touch-point control, and leg-drive timing in competition bench;
  - incline dumbbell bench -> pressing-strength outcome without compromising competition-bench recovery.
- Do not retain or remove a variation merely because it feels novel, difficult, enjoyable, or previously worked. Judge it against the stated purpose and Ren's logged response.

- The flexible day should improve adherence, general conditioning, muscular development, or recovery. It should not become an untracked sixth hard session.
- Reduce or remove cardio during peaking if it measurably worsens lower-body recovery or main-lift performance.
