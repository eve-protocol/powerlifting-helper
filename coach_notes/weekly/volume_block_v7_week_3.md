## Weekly verdict
- **Week 3 produced the strongest squat evidence of the block, restored competition-bench confidence after an isolated poor paused-bench day, and exposed a meaningful deadlift realization problem.** It was productive, but not disciplined enough to call every Week 4 benchmark secure.
- Low bar was the clearest success. Source-rate video showed stable rep-six speed and chest/hip timing at 147.5kg; 150kg landed at the ceiling; Saturday light work remained easy.
- Bench varied by movement and day rather than being uniformly weak. Paused bench was suppressed Monday, incline remained slightly hot Tuesday, competition bench recovered Friday, and controlled Spoto closed the week positively.
- Deadlift capacity remains evident, but top-end expression is unreliable. Paused work progressed, yet regular 172.5kg sumo produced one failed fifth and one @9 fifth with a reported top-half grind after four fast reps.
- Execution discipline improved in some main-lift calls but remained inconsistent. Wednesday squat and Saturday Spoto were well autoregulated; rows, leg curls, pull-ups, Friday pressing backoffs, extra reps, and the revenge deadlift retry still spent avoidable fatigue.

## Lift-by-lift trend
- **Squat:** Strong upward trajectory. The 147.5-150kg sixes support 160kg x5, and video showed desired chest/hip timing. Exact competition depth was angle-limited. High-bar progression is less clear because the movement changed from ATG to competition depth.
- **Bench:** Rep strength remains sufficient for 122.5kg x5, but readiness and repeated-set management are inconsistent. Competition-specific 115kg x6 @7.5 is the best positive signal; Monday and Tuesday require strict warm-up gates rather than benchmark cancellation.
- **Deadlift:** Mixed and now the main concern. Paused 160kg showed capacity, but Saturday showed fast early reps followed by failed or grinding fifth reps at 172.5kg. This supports the active upper-back/bar-connection hypothesis; video is needed to identify the visible mechanism.
- **Incline dumbbell bench:** 50kg x6 is available but above the accumulation target. The Week 4 50kg x8 @8.5-9 goal is possible but not assured.
- **Pull-ups/accessories:** Pull-ups progressed to repeatable +27.5kg fives with small final-set drift. Leg extensions progressed cleanly. T-bar rows and leg curls were the largest accessory overshoots; counting accuracy and late reductions remained inconsistent.

## Biggest win
- Low-bar squat transferred cleanly into heavier six-rep work. Load progression, controlled RPEs, and reviewed torso timing make 160kg x5 the best-supported Week 4 benchmark.

## Biggest limiter
- Deadlift force cannot yet be expressed consistently through the final top half of a five-rep set. Four fast reps at 172.5kg were followed by a failed fifth and then an @9 fifth that reportedly became a knee-to-lockout battle.

## Week 4 checkpoint interpretation
- Low-bar 160kg x5 below the prior @10 result is supported. Attempt 162.5kg only if warm-ups and torso timing clearly earn it.
- Competition bench 122.5kg x5 remains plausible, with settled unrack, pause, and right-left symmetry as mandatory gates.
- Incline dumbbell 50kg x8 @8.5-9 remains plausible but is a genuine benchmark; 50kg x6 reached @8 this week.
- Sumo 180kg x5 @9-9.5 is conditional rather than presumed. Tuesday's paused 165kg work and Saturday warm-up lockouts must show connected, stacked mechanics. Reduce the attempt if the top half deteriorates before benchmark load.

## Week 4 guardrails
- Monday and Tuesday reduce volume to reveal performance; do not replace removed volume with bonus work.
- High bar: choose and retain one depth standard for every work rep.
- Bench: a ceiling set is a reduction signal, not permission to repeat until a grind appears.
- Tuesday paused sumo is technical practice, not a confidence test: pause at the top of the kneecap, keep the bar touching the legs, hold knees fixed, and finish stacked.
- Review the 2026-08-15 second-set video at source rate if supplied before finalizing Saturday's benchmark gate.
- No revenge attempts after a miss. A failed benchmark or repeated technical failure changes the next decision; it does not require immediate proof that strength still exists.
