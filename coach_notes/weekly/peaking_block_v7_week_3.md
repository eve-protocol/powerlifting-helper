## Programming audit
- The peaking structure is still appropriate, but the execution guardrails must tighten for Week 4.
- Squat has followed the intended progression well enough: weekly heavy singles moved from 165kg @8 to 170kg @9 to 172.5kg @9. The next step is not more load practice; it is opener precision.
- Bench is ready enough if the setup is normal. Do not let travel-gym data override the clean Week 2 signal.
- Deadlift cannot be selected from floor speed alone. The main error risk is assuming a fast 180kg means a 195-200kg pull is available. Week 3 shows the opposite: the floor can be fast while the lockout still fails the attempt.
- Backoff and support work should be reduced aggressively if the heavy pull becomes a max-effort top-half grind. The 175kg double became useful only because Ren shifted focus to hips through and did not keep pulling afterward.

## Week 4 implications
- SBD opener day should be conservative and technical. It is not another test day.
- Squat opener: choose a load that allows chest/hips together and knees forward. The Week 3 single supports a normal opener path, but 180kg on mock-meet day should require a clean opener and second attempt.
- Bench opener: use normal-gym setup and the 132.5kg x1 @8 signal as the anchor. Avoid reacting to the unreliable 2026-07-15 travel-gym singles.
- Deadlift opener: prioritize lockout quality. A clean 180-185kg opener is more valuable than a faster floor pull that finishes with soft knees and rounded-forward chest. The 200kg goal should require clean lockout on opener and second attempt; if the same top-half pattern appears, cap below 200kg.
- Support work in Week 4 should be boring. Pull-ups, light upper, and technique sessions should preserve readiness, not add proof work.

## Risks to carry forward
- Deadlift top-half position is the largest risk for the mock meet. The cue that helped was "hips through before it slows," but it must happen earlier than mid-thigh.
- Squat can still drift into back/hip dominance when fatigue appears, especially after heavy pulling.
- Bench is strong enough to tempt aggressive attempts, but normal setup and clear pauses matter more than confidence.
- Travel and equipment noise made Week 3 less clean than ideal. Week 4 should rebuild reliability, not chase what was missed in Thailand.

## Verdict
Week 3 supports the Peaking Block v7 goals with stricter conditions. Squat progressed and keeps 180kg realistic. Bench remains healthy when judged by clean setup data. Deadlift still has enough strength to keep the 200kg path alive in theory, but today's top-half execution argues for conservative gates: no 200kg attempt unless the opener and second attempt lock cleanly. The main Week 4 job is simple: make the attempts look like meet lifts, not rescue projects.
