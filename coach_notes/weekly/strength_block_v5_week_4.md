## Week 4 objectives
- Convert the block's lower-body rebuild into benchmark results without abandoning technical gates.
- Squat: hit 165kg x3 @9-9.5 with better chest/hip timing than prior heavy triples.
- Bench: hit 130kg x3 @9-9.5 only if setup and heavy-bar groove held.
- Sumo deadlift: hit 185kg x3 @9-9.5 only if lat/lockout position held.
- Keep support work from stealing recovery or reinforcing the old position faults.

## Current trajectory
- Overall: mixed, not failed. Squat achieved the block target. Bench and deadlift missed narrowly, and both misses exposed specific execution/process limits rather than a lack of general strength.
- Squat: successful. Week 4 low-bar reached 165kg x3 @9.5, meeting the Strength Block v5 goal. The limiting pattern remains rep-3 hip/back takeover, but the load result moved forward.
- Bench: near miss. 130kg x2 @9.5 and 125kg x3 @9.5 did not meet the 130kg x3 goal. Pressing base still looks strong, but heavy setup/unrack/triple specificity did not convert.
- Deadlift: near miss and position-limited. 185kg x2 @9.5 plus 180kg x3 @10 did not meet the 185kg x3 goal. Video showed the same top-half issue: the bar breaks easily, then lockout becomes chest/neck/back extension before the hips meet the bar.
- Accessories/support work: better controlled than some prior weeks, but still not consistently disciplined. Pull-ups, lateral raises, arms, and some support pressing drifted above target. Saturday's post-deadlift squat also overshot the intended light technical slot.

## Main evidence
- Paused squat: 140kg x2 @7 and 142.5kg x2 @7.5. Strong positional bridge into the low-bar benchmark.
- Paused bench: 112.5kg x2 @6.5 and 115kg x2 @7.5. Useful support work, slightly high on set 2.
- Paused sumo: 167.5kg x2 @6.5 and 170kg x2 @6.5. Strong evidence that the start and initial pull were not the deadlift limiter.
- Low-bar squat: 157.5kg x1, 165kg x3 @9.5, and 152.5kg x3 @7.5. The block's clearest win.
- Bench press: 130kg x2 @9.5, 125kg x3 @9.5, and 100kg x12 @8.5. Close to the goal, but not converted.
- Sumo deadlift: 180kg x1 @7, 185kg x2 @9.5, and 180kg x3 @10. Close to the goal, but the video makes the lockout-position miss clear.
- Larsen press: 115kg x3 @7.5 and 115kg x3 @7 after the heavy bench miss. Pressing support strength remains good.

## Programming audit
- Calibration singles were overused or misapplied for benchmark triples. The worst pattern was prescribing or considering near-workload singles immediately before the set that was supposed to express the benchmark. This added fatigue and decision noise without reliably improving attempt selection.
- Future benchmark triples should use normal warm-ups plus one clearly submaximal bridge single only when it gives information without stealing the attempt. Do not take the target load for a single immediately before trying the target load for reps unless the explicit goal is single practice.
- The low-bar / high-bar balance deserves adjustment. The active coaching focus wanted less posterior-chain-dominant squatting, but the block still relied heavily on low-bar expression. More high-bar or paused-squat development work likely fits the rebuild thesis better, while low-bar should stay specific enough to test and peak.
- The bench miss is less clearly about Spoto vs Larsen. The stronger explanation is heavy setup/unrack and heavy-triple specificity. Spoto could be useful, but the next cycle should first solve how heavy bench is unracked, settled, touched, and pressed under load.
- Deadlift assistance should shift from more floor-breaking strength to top-half position. The next development cycle should prioritize paused sumo at knee, just-below-knee block pulls, and RDLs over more generic heavy sumo volume.

## Risks to carry forward
- Deadlift lockout can look strong off the floor while failing at the last quarter. Do not overrate floor speed when the actual limiter is bar-body connection above the knee.
- Squat can hit the target load while still reinforcing the old hip/back solution on final reps. Future progress should require cleaner rep-3 mechanics, not only heavier triples.
- Bench heavy-bar confidence and setup remain the limiter. The pressing base is not the main problem.
- Support work still has a tendency to become proof-of-strength work. During peaking, this must be tightened.

## Peaking block implications
- Squat: the 165kg x3 @9.5 supports the peaking block's 180kg goal, but the route should be cleaner singles, not more heavy triples. Keep the cue "knees forward until the bar moves."
- Bench: do not assume 130kg x3 strength is present. Peak through cleaner heavy singles and setup practice; use heavy triples sparingly and below miss territory.
- Deadlift: peak the lockout. Singles should teach legal, stacked finishes with ribs down and the bar glued to the thighs. Avoid max-effort rep work that reinforces lean-back lockouts.
- Assistance during the peak should be minimal and targeted: enough to keep positions alive, not enough to create new fatigue.

## Verdict
Strength Block v5 produced one clear benchmark win and two honest near misses. Squat moved forward. Bench and deadlift did not hit their headline targets, but the misses gave specific information: bench needs heavy setup/triple execution, and deadlift needs top-half position rather than more floor strength. The biggest programming lesson is that singles before benchmark triples need stricter rules. The next peaking block should express strength through cleaner singles while avoiding the support-work and calibration choices that turned Week 4 into more fatigue than necessary.
