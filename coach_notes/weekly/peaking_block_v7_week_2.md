- Squat: move the low-bar top single heavier while preserving the rebuild cue well enough to keep the 180kg goal live.

The week produced useful strength signals: 170kg low-bar x1 @9, 132.5kg bench x1 @8, and an easy 180kg deadlift single followed by 185kg x1 @8.5 after a 190kg miss. Those are enough to keep the Week 4 mock-meet goals live, but the week again showed that the main risk is attempt selection and turning planned exposures into proof work.

The Saturday 180kg x1 @6.5 readiness signal was good, but the direct jump to 190kg on a worse bar created an avoidable failed attempt. The later 185kg x1 @8.5 preserves the trajectory but does not cleanly advance it.

## Programming audit
- The programmed structure is working. The week gave repeated specific exposures without excessive volume, and the main lifts all showed enough strength for a peaking trajectory.
- The athlete decision layer is the weak link. Week 1 warned against extra proof work; Week 2 repeated the pattern in a more costly way on deadlift. The block can absorb this once. It should not absorb it again in Week 3.
- Deadlift needs a stricter attempt ladder. Environmental changes such as a thicker, smoother bar must lower aggression. Easy floor speed at 180kg is not enough to justify skipping the bridge when the known limiter is grip/bar connection and top-half completion.
- Squat needs the same bridge rule in Week 3. The 170kg @9 was useful because it was made cleanly and backoffs were controlled, but any jump above 170kg should require warm-up speed and chest/hip timing to be clearly better.
- Bench is the model for the rest of the block. One small adjustment after an easy first single, then stop. That is exactly how peaking should work.

## Risks to carry forward
- Deadlift misses above 185kg can quickly become grip/top-half practice in the wrong direction if attempts are jumped too aggressively.
- Squat can still become hip-led when fatigue rises, especially after heavy or failed pulls.
- Bench confidence is now high enough to be useful, but it still needs the same "singles stay singles" rule.
- Support work can drift hot in small ways; during Week 3 and mock-meet week, those small drifts are not worth it.

## Week 3 implications
- Squat: use 170kg x1 @9 as the anchor, not the floor. A Week 3 increase above 170kg is reasonable only if the bridge single is cleaner and faster than this week's 160/170 sequence. If chest-lag appears early, stay at or below 170kg and preserve the mock-meet path.
- Bench: 132.5kg x1 @8 supports a heavier controlled single next time if warm-ups are sharp. Do not take extra reps. The win is clean attempt practice.
- Deadlift: the next heavy pull must use a bridge. If 180kg is easy again, go 185kg or 187.5kg before considering 190kg. A bad bar or grip uncertainty caps the day lower. The goal is a clean heavy single plus useful backoff work, not proving 190kg after a suspicious setup.
- Paused deadlift: keep 170-172.5kg as the controlled default. 175kg belongs only if the day explicitly calls for the harder end or if 172.5kg is clearly under target with a clean restart.
- Light/support work: keep Monday low-bar and paused bench boring enough to preserve Wednesday/Saturday. Week 3 is not the time to win accessories.

## Verdict
Week 2 supports the Peaking Block v7 goals but narrows the margin for error. Squat and bench both moved forward, and deadlift still has enough strength evidence for the 200kg path. The limiting factor is not readiness; it is whether the heavy attempts are selected in a way that rehearses the mock-meet process instead of creating extra misses. Week 3 should be stricter: bridge singles, no skipped steps because a warm-up feels easy, and no heavier attempt when bar feel, grip, or top-half position is uncertain.
