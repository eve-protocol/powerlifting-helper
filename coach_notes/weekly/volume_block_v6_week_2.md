## Week 4 objectives for this block
- Low-bar squat: 157.5-160kg x5 @9-9.5, with less hip-rise / chest-lag than the previous 155kg x5 @9 baseline.
- Sumo deadlift: 177.5kg x5 @9-9.5, with cleaner lockout/top-half position than previous heavy rep work.
- Secondary objective: keep bench progressing without letting bench/pull/accessory overshoots compromise the lower-body rebuild.

## Current trajectory
- Overall: on track, with deadlift now the clearest positive signal of the block. Week 2 showed enough strength to keep the Week 4 targets realistic, but the same technical caveat remains: progress only counts if the heavier work does not drift back into the old hips-first/back-dominant pattern.
- Squat: on track but still conditional. The heavy low-bar day produced a strong signal with 142.5kg x6 @7-8, which supports a 157.5-160kg x5 Week 4 target. The limiting pattern is not strength; it is keeping the bottom and first inch organized, especially preventing chest-lag / hip-lead and right/left asymmetry as load rises.
- Deadlift: strongly on track. Week 1's 160kg 3x6 @6.5-7 progressed to Week 2's 167.5kg x5 @7 after two 165kg sets. That makes 177.5kg x5 @9-9.5 in Week 4 clearly realistic. The top half and lockouts are cleaner than the failed/mock-meet pattern, but the first-inch wedge still needs attention.
- Bench: ahead of expectation. Competition bench 112.5kg x6 @7-7.5 and Larsen 110kg x6 @7.5 show pressing strength is moving well. The only block-management issue is not letting bench accessories or Saturday Larsen become a recovery sink for the lower-body goals.
- Accessories: still the least controlled part of the week. Pull-ups, chest fly, triceps, curls, rear delt fly, and leg curl all had some RPE spikes. This is not a primary concern, but accessory loading should be based on the expected second or third set, not the easiest first set.

## Main evidence
- High bar squat: 117.5kg x6 @6, then 120kg x6 @7 and @7.5. Productive for knee-forward/high-bar practice, though later reps still wanted to revert toward hip-first mechanics.
- Paused bench: 102.5kg x5 @8 overshot because Ren's true paused bench is a long 0-1-2-3 count. Dropping to 100kg kept the remaining work productive.
- Paused sumo deadlift: 155kg x4 @6, 160kg x4 @7, 157.5kg x4 @6.5. Progressed from Week 1 and reinforced that 160kg is available when the target allows @7, but wedge quality remains the deciding factor.
- Heavy low-bar squat: 140kg x6 @6, 142.5kg x6 @7, 142.5kg x6 @8. This is a clear strength signal and likely a 6RM PR, but the final set was a mild overshoot and showed the same hip-lead/asymmetry pattern.
- Competition bench: 112.5kg x6 @7, 112.5kg x6 @7.5, 110kg x6 @7.5. Strong and productive; the drop to 110kg protected recovery.
- Sumo deadlift: 165kg x5 @7, 165kg x5 @6.5, 167.5kg x5 @7. This is the best Week 2 signal and strongly supports the Week 4 deadlift objective.
- Light low-bar after deadlift: 135kg x5 x2. First set had balance noise; second set improved when Ren slowed the descent. This supports the squat technical theme: controlled descent, midfoot pressure, chest and hips together.
- Larsen press: 107.5kg x6 @6, 110kg x6 @7.5. Pressing power was underestimated; 110kg is validated as an upper-end working load for this slot.

## Risks to manage in Week 3
- Do not let the good deadlift signal turn into aggressive jumps. Week 3 deadlift should likely begin around 170kg for 5s if warm-ups are normal, then only move up if wedge and lockout stay clean.
- Low-bar squat load should continue to be earned by position. 142.5kg is validated; 145kg is available only if the first set does not immediately look like @8 or worsen the asymmetry/hip-lead pattern.
- The post-deadlift light squat should stay technical. Use the slower descent strategy if 135kg is used again; reduce if balance becomes the limiting factor.
- Bench can handle more than originally estimated, but the lower-body rebuild is still the primary block goal. Do not turn Larsen or accessories into hidden high-fatigue work.
- Accessory RPE spikes are recurring. Start lighter or plan drops earlier, especially for pull-ups, triceps, curls, leg curls, and fly variations.

## Week 3 coaching directive
- Deadlift: allow progression, but cap it by position. Main cue: slack out, lats locked, push floor apart before hips climb. If the first inch becomes hips-first, hold or reduce load even if the bar speed is acceptable.
- Squat: keep using the technical strategy that worked: squat shoes, controlled descent, midfoot pressure, and chest/hips rising together. Load only counts if it supports the Week 4 cleaner-5RM objective.
- Bench: raise Larsen expectations modestly. 110kg is a real working load, but use it to hit the target rather than to chase a Saturday bench PR.
- Accessories: choose loads for stable repeated sets, not for the first set. Preserve recovery for the Week 4 squat/deadlift tests.

## Bottom line
Week 2 advanced the block. Deadlift is now clearly on track for 177.5kg x5 @9-9.5 in Week 4, and squat strength is also on track if the position work holds. The important coaching constraint did not change: the goal is not just heavier 5s, it is heavier 5s with less lower-body technical compensation than the pre-block baselines.
