- Keep squat chest/hip timing, bench setup and right-left symmetry, and deadlift knee-to-lockout connection subordinate to load alone.

## Weekly verdict
- **Week 2 clearly progressed accumulation strength, but execution discipline improved only partially.** Every required session was completed and the main movement anchors moved up. Deadlift work was the cleanest success; bench and incline showed strong opening capacity but repeated costly final-set overshoots.
- The week strengthens the evidence that Ren is highly capable in the five- to seven-rep range. It still does not prove transfer to the Week 4 fives, later triples, or mock-meet singles. That distinction remains central after the previous cycle's failed realization.
- Compared with Week 1, load adjustments were more common and generally more effective. High bar, pull-ups, rows, lateral raises, Friday fly work, Saturday sumo, and Saturday Spoto all included useful corrections. The remaining problem is that several corrections came only after the target ceiling had already been exceeded substantially.

## Lift-by-lift trend
- **Squat:** Both squat patterns progressed. ATG high bar rose to 115kg for sevens with only a small second-set overshoot, and low bar rose from 132.5kg x8 work to 140kg x7 work. The final 140kg set at @8 and light 137.5kg finish at @6.5 show normal fatigue drift, not regression. Week 3's heavier sixes must be judged by chest/hip timing, not completion alone.
- **Bench:** Rep strength remains a major positive signal. Two competition-bench sets at 112.5kg x7 and controlled Spoto at 102.5kg x7 support the 122.5kg x5 checkpoint trajectory. The limiter was fatigue management: paused bench finished @8, incline finished @9.5, competition bench finished @9.5, and triceps reached @9. These overshoots reduce confidence in repeating fixed loads across all sets, not in the underlying strength.
- **Deadlift:** Best trend of the week. Paused sumo improved from 150kg to 155kg for three controlled fives, and regular sumo improved from 160kg x6 to 167.5kg x6 at the same top RPE. The Saturday backoff restored target effort instead of creating a final-set grind. Capacity supports the 180kg x5 path, but no Week 2 video verified the active pass condition of a connected, stacked lockout.
- **Incline dumbbell bench:** 47.5kg x7 for three sets is a genuine progression toward 52.5kg x6, but the @8, @8, @9.5 sequence was too costly for an accumulation day. In Week 3, 50kg should be treated as a first-set test, not an automatic repeated load.
- **Pull-ups and accessories:** Pull-up strength progressed enough to test +25kg, but +22.5kg remains the repeatable six-rep anchor. Leg extensions, leg curls, rear delts, and cable crunches progressed productively. T-bar rows, lateral raises, arms, and fly work still showed preventable overshoots or extra work.

## Biggest win
- Deadlift progression without a late-session failure. The week combined technically specific paused work at 155kg with regular sumo at 167.5kg, and Saturday's final reduction restored the target rather than letting the session drift upward. This is the clearest evidence that the accumulation plan is moving the 180kg x5 checkpoint forward.

## Biggest limiter
- Reductions still occur too late. Incline bench, competition bench, triceps, rows, pull-ups, and some squat work exceeded their ceilings after earlier sets had already warned that the fixed load was no longer repeatable. Accidental extra reps and an extra curl set also persisted. The issue is not insufficient effort; it is spending fatigue after the useful training signal has already been obtained.

## Block checkpoint interpretation
- Low-bar 160kg x5 below the prior @10 remains plausible. Week 2's 140kg sevens support the path, but Week 3's 147.5kg sixes must retain chest/hip timing before the benchmark is considered earned.
- Bench 122.5kg x5 remains plausible. The first two 112.5kg x7 sets are strong evidence, but the @9.5 finish and repeated pressing overshoots mean Week 3 must preserve setup and repeatability rather than chase confidence.
- Incline dumbbell 52.5kg x6 @8.5-9 remains plausible, but 50kg x6 in Week 3 must first be managed as controlled work rather than three forced sets.
- Sumo 180kg x5 @9-9.5 is supported by the 167.5kg x6 result. The checkpoint still requires legal connected lockout mechanics; RPE and floor speed alone cannot pass it.

## Week 3 guardrails
- Week 3 is the strongest full-volume week, not a test week. Use each written anchor as a first-set proposal and reduce as soon as the set reaches the ceiling.
- High-bar 122.5kg x6, paused bench 110kg x5, low-bar 147.5kg x6, competition bench 115kg x6, and sumo 172.5kg x5 are justified directions, but none must be repeated across all sets after an overshoot.
- Incline dumbbell 50kg x6 is a first-set test. If it exceeds @7.5, return to 47.5kg immediately.
- Pull-ups: +27.5kg x5 is a test anchor. Do not infer from one successful set that it belongs on all three sets.
- Deadlift cue: lats tight, pull from the loaded position, stay connected through the knee, and finish stacked. Do not add another hip sink.
- Squat cue: knees forward for the first inch; chest and hips rise together.
- Bench cue: settle in the final position, preserve the hover or pause standard, then drive back into the bench.
- No bonus reps or bonus sets. Week 3 succeeds when the strongest prescribed week remains repeatable enough to reveal real transfer in Week 4.
