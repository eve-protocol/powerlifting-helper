## Programming audit
- The block failed its outcome criteria. The active coaching focus says success requires meeting or beating active block goals without exceeding target RPE, or showing the same load at lower RPE/cleaner position. Week 4 did neither for squat or deadlift.
- The RPE-based weekly signals were too optimistic for realization. Week 3 made 180 squat and 200 deadlift look theoretically alive, but the mock meet showed those calls were not robust to real-day execution.
- The program identified the right technical problems but did not create enough transfer. Squat still failed to express heavier low-bar strength. Deadlift still failed at lockout, exactly where the block said it needed to improve.
- The taper/realization structure did reduce volume, but it did not produce a performance peak. Week 4 was light enough on paper, yet the athlete did not arrive with improved max performance.
- Travel, health issues, Achilles sensation, elbow/tendon context, and compressed sessions all matter. But they are load-management problems the program had to absorb or adapt to, not reasons to count the block as successful.

## Block verdict
- Peaking Block v7 was not successful.
- 12-week arc result: 510kg on 2026-05-02 to 500kg on 2026-07-26.
- Squat result: 175kg to 172.5kg, with 180kg missed.
- Bench result: 140kg to 137.5kg, with 142.5kg missed.
- Deadlift result: 195kg to 190kg, with 195kg missed at lockout.
- The squat/deadlift rebuild thesis remains valid, but the programming implementation failed Ren's stated expectation and the block's objective metrics.

## Risks to carry forward
- Do not explain this away as a bad day. Ren explicitly flagged the result as programming failure and that assessment is supported by the outcome data.
- Do not rely on late-cycle RPE singles as proof that a PR is ready. A single can be plausible without being realizable on mock-meet day.
- Deadlift floor speed will continue to over-predict deadlift performance until lockout is fixed.
- Squat can show good submaximal technique without transferring to a heavier single if the program does not force the right adaptation.

## Next cycle requirements
- Start with a full 12-week review before writing the next block.
- Define objective checkpoints for squat and deadlift, not just end goals.
- If checkpoints fail, change the plan early rather than carrying the same assumptions to the mock meet.
- Build the next program around measurable transfer: heavier low-bar singles/doubles with the required position, deadlift lockout work that actually changes heavy-sumo finish mechanics, and attempt-selection rules tied to demonstrated performance rather than target optimism.
