## Weekly verdict
- **Week 1 was successful as accumulation, but transfer is unproven.** The main barbell work was completed and generally repeatable. Ren clearly expresses strength well through moderate and higher rep ranges.
- The week strongly confirms a performance-expression gap. Current 5-9 rep performance looks disproportionately good compared with the prior block's heavy results: squat 165kg x3 @9.5 and 172.5kg x1 @9.5; bench 125kg x3 @9.5 and 137.5kg x1 @9.5; deadlift 180kg x3 @10 and 190kg x1 @9.
- One week cannot show that the program has created a new adaptation. It establishes the baseline from which later transfer must be judged.

## Lift-by-lift read
- **Squat:** Best-controlled pattern of the week. Both low-bar exposures landed well, and the light post-deadlift work was especially easy. The risk is assuming that clean volume automatically means the old 172.5-180kg single problem is solved. Continue knees first, then keep chest and hips together as loading rises.
- **Bench:** Rep strength is excellent. Competition bench at 107.5kg for 3 x8 is the strongest positive signal of the week. Paused and Spoto work also support the base. However, right-side sensation history and the previous 137.5kg mock-meet result mean heavy transfer must still be demonstrated.
- **Deadlift:** Conventional volume strength was good, but technique remains the clearest limiter. The paused-deadlift cue experiment caused an RPE spike, while Saturday's source-rate footage showed the actual issue begins in the wedge: hips are set slightly below the effective start and rise before bar break. Keep the lats connected and pull from the position created after slack is removed; do not add another hip sink.

## Main win
- Ren completed a large amount of productive competition-pattern work without primary-lift failure. The week provides a strong, credible accumulation baseline and confirms that muscular endurance and repeatability are not the current bottleneck.

## Main limiter
- Execution discipline, not work capacity. Easy openers repeatedly encouraged extra reps, aggressive jumps, delayed reductions, or bonus sets. Several accessories reached RPE 8.5-10, and the third paused-sumo set became a cue experiment at RPE 9.5. These choices add fatigue without improving the transfer objective.

## Checkpoint interpretation
- Ren's original purpose for checkpoints was to support beating prior 3RM and 1RM performances. That remains the governing goal.
- The Week 4 accumulation checkpoints are not direct max attempts because they occur while building volume. They are intermediate transfer gates: cleaner or stronger fives should justify progression into heavier triples, and the later strength/peaking blocks must ultimately beat the prior heavy benchmarks.
- Checkpoint success cannot be defined as merely completing a prescribed load. It must show better load, lower RPE, cleaner position, or enough robustness to support the next heavy phase.
- The relevant Week 4 gates remain: squat 160kg x5 cleaner than the prior maximal effort, bench 122.5kg x5 completed, incline dumbbell 52.5kg x6 @8.5-9, and sumo 180kg x5 @9-9.5 with clean lockouts. Later planned triples and singles must then verify actual transfer toward 182.5/145/202.5 and a 530kg total.

## Carry forward to Week 2
- Primary sets: follow the written anchor and RPE. An easy first set permits confidence, not automatic escalation.
- Accessories: no bonus sets. Reduce immediately after an overshoot.
- Squat cue: knees first; chest and hips together.
- Bench cue: settle, pause or hover, press back.
- Deadlift cue: lats tight—pull from there. No extra sink after taking slack.
- Weekly success condition: preserve the strong rep performance while reducing avoidable RPE spikes. That gives the later checkpoints the best chance to reveal real transfer.
