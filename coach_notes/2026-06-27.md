## Coaching assessment
- This was a deadlift benchmark miss, but not a broad deadlift strength collapse. The planned Strength Block v5 goal was 185kg x3 @9-9.5 if lat/lockout position held. The actual result was 185kg x2 @9.5 plus 180kg x3 @10 after a failed retry. That confirms the strength is close, but the benchmark was limited by top-half position and lockout mechanics.
- The 180kg single did its job as a readiness check and was not too heavy by itself. The problem was that the subsequent 185kg attempt exposed exactly the Week 3 risk: above the knee, the lift shifted toward chest/head-back extension instead of hips meeting the bar while the bar stayed pinned in.
- The retry at 185kg was not useful training. It converted a near miss into extra fatigue, then made the later 180kg triple technically worse. Future benchmark handling should treat a failed rep as the end of the top attempt, then take a cleaner backoff or move on.
- The 180kg x3 @10 still matters. It shows 180kg triples are available even under fatigue, but today's execution does not support calling the 185kg triple ready. It should be logged as position-limited rather than simply "not strong enough."
- The low-bar support work overshot the planned target slightly. Today's squat prescription was 2 x 2 @5-5.5 after heavy deadlifts; 150kg x2 x2 was logged around @5.5-6 and came with stiffness/depth uncertainty. Because the goal was technical support, 140-145kg would have been the cleaner call once depth felt hard to judge.
- Larsen press was slightly above target on set 1 but acceptable overall. 115kg x3 x2 after the hard Thursday bench day was strong enough, but this should be read as support work, not a reason to keep pressing intensity high in the peak.

## Block trajectory impact
- Strength Block v5 ended with a mixed but informative Week 4: squat benchmark achieved, bench benchmark missed narrowly, deadlift benchmark missed narrowly and was clearly lockout-position limited.
- The active rebuild thesis is validated. Squat and deadlift strength are present, but heavy-effort reps still default toward posterior-chain/back-dominant solutions when positions degrade.
- Ren's critique of the block is partly correct. The recurring benchmark-single pattern was not useful enough relative to the fatigue and decision noise it created. For future blocks, calibration singles should either be clearly submaximal and materially lighter than the target set, or skipped in favor of normal warm-ups before the benchmark set.
- The exercise-selection concern is plausible but not proven. High-bar squat likely would have reduced some low-back loading and reinforced the rebuild focus better than repeated low-bar exposures, while Spoto vs Larsen is less clearly responsible for the bench miss than heavy setup/unrack specificity.

## Technical theme to carry forward
- Deadlift: ribs down, bar glued to thighs, stand tall. Do not chase lockout by throwing the head/chest back.
- Squat: depth and knees-forward position matter more than adding support-work load after deadlifts.
- Benchmark execution: one failed rep ends the top attempt; correct with a clean backoff, not a same-load retry.

## Next-time guidance
- Peaking deadlift: keep heavy singles available, but use them as the primary peaking skill rather than as pre-fatigue before rep benchmarks. For top singles, prioritize legal lockout and bar-body connection over speed off the floor.
- Next cycle deadlift development: include paused sumo at knee or just-below-knee block pulls, plus RDLs, to build top-half position without reinforcing lean-back finishes.
- Next cycle squat development: keep more high-bar or paused-squat exposure if the goal is to reduce low-back dominance; low-bar heavy work should be specific and gated by chest/hip timing.
- Next cycle bench development: rebuild the heavy triple through cleaner 125-127.5kg work and better setup/handoff/unrack practice rather than assuming more general pressing volume will solve the 130kg x3 miss.
